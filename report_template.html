First, the futures question

There are no {{name}} futures

Single-stock futures haven't traded in the US since OneChicago closed in 2020, so there is no {{sym}} futures contract to pull. The implied forward embedded in the options chain does the same job. For every expiration, put-call parity pins the price at which the market will transact {{sym}} on that date.

The forecast

Where the distribution goes

From the smoothed volatility smile at each expiration I recover the full risk-neutral density by Breeden–Litzenberger, the second derivative of call price with respect to strike. Interpolating across expirations in variance time puts the earnings jump on the right date. That gives a complete probability distribution at any horizon.

Why the two measures differ

The risk-neutral distribution read straight off option prices is not a forecast. It is drift-free by construction, and it is usually too wide because investors pay a premium for protection. Turning it into a real-world forecast takes two adjustments.

Volatility adjustment. {{calib_text}}

Drift. A 4.12% risk-free rate plus {{name}}'s {{beta}} beta{{beta_note}} against a 4.5% equity risk premium gives {{mu}}% a year, about {{mu_m}}% a month. Over 45 days that is ${{drift45}} on a ${{spot0}} stock, which is why the central estimate hardly moves. At this volatility, drift is noise.

Limits

What this cannot tell you

  • The distribution is conditional on one moment. It prices the information in the market at {{asof}}. A new analyst note, a hyperscaler capex revision or a company pre-announcement would invalidate it immediately.
  • The volatility adjustment is estimated. It comes from past days in a similar volatility regime, which overlap heavily. If the regime is changing, realized volatility can differ sharply from the estimate.
    • {{SCEN_LIMIT}}
  • Fat tails are likely understated. The lognormal-with-skew form underweights a disorderly AI-complex unwind, in which correlations go to one.
  • {{data_limit_head}} {{data_limit}}
  • None of this is a recommendation. It describes what one market is charging for risk at one moment.
Method

How this was built

All {{n_exp}} listed expirations and {{n_contracts}} contracts pulled from Yahoo Finance. {{method_prices}} Forward and discount factor per expiration come from open-interest-weighted put-call parity on near-the-money strikes{{method_fwd}}. Implied volatilities are solved by Brent inversion of Black-76 against that forward, using the out-of-the-money side at every strike. An SVI smile is fitted per expiration, densities are recovered by Breeden–Litzenberger on the smoothed smile, and interpolation across expirations runs in cumulative-variance time so the earnings jump stays on its date. The next earnings date and past report dates come from Yahoo's earnings calendar. Realized volatility, earnings reactions and the volatility calibration use daily closes since {{hist_since}}. Cross-complex moves and correlations use daily closes of {{peer_syms}} alongside {{sym}}.
